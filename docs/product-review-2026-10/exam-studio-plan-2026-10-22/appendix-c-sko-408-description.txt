# Skoolee Exam Studio — flagship product specification for LEAP 2027

**Updated:** 15 October 2026 · **Feature:** F18 / sequence 034 · **Priority:** core commercial product · **Delivery status:** proposed expansion over existing code; acceptance and school-pilot evidence required.

**Product promise:** A school brings its curriculum and teaching context. Skoolee helps it create an exam it can explain, deliver an exam students can complete reliably, and turn reviewed results into useful next learning steps.

The product must be compelling to a teacher in minutes, operationally credible to an exam coordinator, and commercially understandable to a school-group buyer. The founder's ambition is a USD 10 million investment or a potential acquisition; that is a business objective, not a guaranteed outcome or a software acceptance criterion. Build the evidence a buyer or investor can inspect.

**This is the flagship specification in the existing independent feature card.** Reuse the domain implementation owned by the linked cards below; do not create duplicate records, new parent tickets, or a second assessment platform. ## 1. The customer experience we are selling

“Tell Skoolee what your class learned. Review the evidence behind its paper. Rehearse the exam. Run it confidently. See exactly why a mark was awarded and what to teach next.”

A suggested Arabic teacher journey: speak the exam brief, upload the approved syllabus and a previous paper, correct the extracted topics, approve the coverage plan, inspect a questionable item, launch a candidate on a supported restricted device, scan a handwritten answer, approve its marking evidence, and publish an understandable result. The same journey must work in English and Urdu.

The commercial entry point is a **standalone assessment suite that can connect to existing school systems**, as well as a premium Skoolee module. Schools should not have to replace their entire ERP to try the assessment product.

| Buyer/user | Work being removed or improved | Evidence of value |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Teacher | Re-entering syllabus, formatting papers, correcting generated questions, maintaining keys | Minutes to an approved paper including all corrections; reuse in a second assessment |
| Exam coordinator | Manual readiness checks, scattered incidents, uncertain saves and submissions | Readiness completion, successful receipts, intervention time and support effort |
| Moderator | Reading every script without priorities, finding evidence, handling regrades | Marking/review time, agreement and override rates, appeal resolution time |
| Principal/school group | Inconsistent curriculum coverage, unclear quality, fragmented reports | Reviewed coverage, defect resolution, repeat adoption and campus-level operating cost |
| Student/family | Confusing instructions, lost work, opaque marks and generic feedback | Successful completion, accessible delivery, understandable evidence and reviewed practice |

End-to-end authoring, delivery and marking already exist in established assessment products. Our differentiation hypothesis is the combination of **school-specific evidence, Arabic-first quality, rehearsal before release, reliable mixed paper/digital delivery, and an explainable improvement loop**. Validate that hypothesis with actual school buyers; do not claim to be the first or only platform.

## 3. Signature capabilities and acceptance

### A. A brief that understands the school

Teacher starts with text, push-to-talk voice, document upload, camera scan, a previous paper or an existing blueprint. Skoolee assembles a visible, editable assessment brief: curriculum/version, grade, subject, taught objectives, excluded material, marks, duration, item mix, language, allowed aids, accommodations, reviewers and delivery mode.

* Ask one meaningful question at a time only when a required fact is missing or contradictory. Offer a structured “edit everything” view.
* Distinguish school-confirmed facts, extracted suggestions and unanswered decisions; show the original page or transcript behind each extracted fact.
* Compare proposed coverage with teacher-confirmed taught topics. Do not assume a scheduled lesson was actually taught.
* A past paper supplies a permitted structure/style reference; it must not silently define the current syllabus or authorize copying.
* Acceptance: ambiguous dates, incompatible mark totals, untaught topics and unsupported objectives produce a precise decision request before generation. Settings survive interruption. Voice transcripts and OCR remain editable.

### B. A question with an inspectable history

Every item has a compact evidence view: “Why this question?”, source page/region, objective, assessed skill, marks, solution/rubric, author/model provenance, reviewers and revision history.

* Separate a directly sourced fact from an original problem derived from an approved concept. A new arithmetic example need not pretend its numbers were quoted from a textbook.
* Teacher-authored objectives require explicit authorization and curriculum review; they cannot be an automatic escape hatch for unsupported AI content.
* Clicking the evidence returns the exact source revision, including the reviewed figure crop. Missing or revoked evidence blocks new approval.
* Acceptance: source → objective → item → rubric → paper version → answer → moderated mark is navigable with role-appropriate access. A citation is evidence of traceability, not proof that the question is correct.

### C. Rehearse the exam before students see it

A “Rehearse this exam” action opens a concise list of issues to resolve, with a student preview beside the paper.

* Combine deterministic checks, independent key-blind answerability review, figure/asset completeness, candidate-versus-key separation, print pagination, keyboard navigation and a real synthetic save/submit rehearsal.
* Show an intentionally ambiguous item, explain the ambiguity and allow targeted repair. An item edit invalidates its dependent answer, rubric and approval.
* Review estimated reading/workload time as an estimate with teacher correction; no invented prediction of actual pupil speed.
* Acceptance: every blocker has evidence, an owner and a repair path. No opaque “99% quality score” substitutes for checks. LLM-generated student simulations are not evidence of calibrated difficulty, fairness or measured learning.

### D. Arabic-first papers and controlled multilingual versions

Offer English, Arabic and Urdu authoring, bilingual teacher review and optional approved parallel language versions.

* Put original and translated item, key and rubric side by side. Flag changed meaning, negation, units, notation, terminology and mark allocation for educator review.
* Freeze each approved language/form version and its relationship to the shared blueprint. Translation changes require renewed review; do not live-translate a high-stakes paper during an attempt.
* Keep exam content language separate from interface language. Use approved bilingual glossaries where the school supplies them.
* Acceptance: RTL prose, LTR mathematics, numbers, Arabic shaping, Urdu typography, tables and diagrams remain readable in browser and print. No claim of equivalent language difficulty without validation.

### E. Controlled parallel forms and exposure-aware reuse

Create form A/B/C within an approved blueprint, with transparent changes and overlap.

* Preserve objectives, marks, question types, allowed aids and scoring structure. Pin teacher-chosen items; use reviewed parameter variation only where the answer/rubric can be revalidated.
* Track when an item/key was released, reuse history and audience scope. Suggest rotation or retirement based on the school's policy.
* Assign and freeze a candidate's form; reload, device recovery and scanning must resolve to that same form.
* Acceptance: the comparison shows shared/changed items and coverage differences. Variants cannot be labeled equally difficult or psychometrically equivalent without empirical calibration. Secure print copies may carry minimal candidate/form identifiers under school policy.

### F. Skoolee Exam Room

This is the student's dedicated exam experience, with **Managed Exam Mode** for the requested restricted-device behavior. Detailed rules are in section 4.

The normal flow is: rehearse device → verify candidate/configuration → start once → answer with visible saves → submit or reach deadline → receive receipt and leave the exam environment. The student should not have to understand security configuration or solve technical settings during the timed exam.

### G. One exam across paper and digital delivery

The approved blueprint and paper versions drive printable packs, online attempts and scanned scripts.

* A teacher selects the permitted delivery path before release. Print and browser use the same frozen questions, marks, approved figures and candidate projection.
* QR/reference matching identifies the school, exam/form, candidate and page sequence through scoped opaque identifiers; no sensitive data is unnecessarily embedded.
* Duplicate/missing pages, wrong forms and uncertain handwriting are held for review. Always show original evidence next to recognition.
* A supervised switch from device to paper records an incident, medium change, used time, current version and last accepted answers. It must not create a second independent marked attempt.
* Acceptance: a reviewed handoff resolves overlap explicitly and never silently combines conflicting answers or changes the timer. General unsupervised full-offline testing is not part of the first pilot.

### H. Mark by exceptions, with evidence

The moderator starts with issues that need judgment: unreadable answer, rubric conflict, unexpected valid solution, disputed step or uncertain proposal.

* Each proposed score points to the answer region/text and rubric criterion, with reason and uncertainty. Deterministic objective scoring is separated from AI-assisted written scoring.
* Calibrate a marking run against teacher-reviewed anchor scripts; disagreements go to a human. A different valid method must not be penalized just because it differs from the generated solution.
* Support anonymized review where school policy requires it, reviewer assignment, override reasons and a second review for selected high-impact decisions.
* Acceptance: humans approve consequential results and release; suggestions never silently become final marks. Repeated import/approval cannot create duplicate marks.

### I. Resolve a faulty question without losing trust

An exam coordinator can report an ambiguous or incorrect item during or after delivery.

* Show affected paper versions, forms, language variants, candidates and downstream results.
* During an active attempt, preserve the original paper; an authorized, versioned clarification reaches the defined affected audience. Never secretly rewrite only some candidates' questions.
* After the attempt, preview policy-based remedies such as rubric correction, regrade or item neutralization; a human authorizes the exact impact before revised results are published.
* Acceptance: every change records reason, approver, old/new result versions and notification decision. AI cannot unilaterally adjust marks, exam duration or publication.

### J. Results that lead to the next useful action

The learner sees “what I understood, where my method needs work, and what to practice next” after authorized publication.

* Link feedback to their actual moderated answer and approved objective, not a generic generated paragraph.
* Teacher receives suggested reteaching groups and short practice activities; guardians receive only permitted, age-appropriate released information.
* Later formative checks show observed changes over time with sample counts. Practice questions must not leak future secured exam material.
* Acceptance: recommendations remain reviewable; do not infer diagnosis, intelligence, teacher quality or causal learning gain from a score trend. High-stakes adaptive exams are a separate research phase.

### K. A control room that makes exam day manageable

Provide an invigilator view of verified readiness, active attempts, last acknowledged save, pending connection incidents, approved accommodations, support requests and receipts.

* Show factual operational signals with last update time; a stale heartbeat is not proof of misconduct.
* Give precise actions for approved recovery, extra time within the published rules, supervised interruption and release. Preview affected candidates and require reason/audit.
* Surface outages at the correct school/campus/room scope and provide a rehearsed fallback.
* Acceptance: an invigilator sees no answer keys or unrestricted student records merely because they can supervise. Bulk actions have scope confirmation and idempotent results.

### L. An evidence pack a school can explain

An authorized reviewer can follow the curriculum and approvals through the exact delivered form, response revisions, rubric decisions, result release and appeal.

* Exports contain version references, timestamps, reviewer decisions and a verifiable integrity manifest; access and export activity are audited.
* Exports follow school retention and student/guardian access rules. A content hash does not establish a student's identity, correctness of a mark or proof of cheating.
* Acceptance: disputed results can be reconstructed without piecing together chats and screenshots, and cross-school access tests fail safely.

## 4. Required restricted-browser mode

### Decision and terminology

The requested “private browser” is now specified as a **dedicated, policy-controlled exam client**, not Chrome/Safari incognito. Propose integrating a maintained client such as Safe Exam Browser through [SKO-449 — Safe Exam Browser integration](<https://linear.app/skoolee-ai-app/issue/SKO-449>), rather than building an operating-system security agent for the initial release. Client selection remains subject to compatibility, licensing, maintenance and independent security review.

| Delivery mode | Intended experience | Admission rule |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Standard browser | Focused Skoolee session for school-approved standard/mocks/open-resource assessment | App routes/actions restricted during attempt; no device-lockdown claim |
| Managed Exam Mode | Required restricted session on an explicitly supported device/client configuration | Validate supported client/configuration and exam policy before issuing the restricted candidate session |
| Approved supervised alternative | School-controlled paper or supported supervised device for accessibility/technical needs | Coordinator records the approved alternative and attempt continuity; no silent downgrade |

**For an exam published as Managed, an ordinary or private browser must not start the timer or retrieve the protected paper.** Unsupported devices get a preparation/alternative route before the exam; they are not given a misleading “secure” badge.

### Admission and enforcement

* School publishes a versioned delivery policy: supported OS/client versions, resource allowlist, permitted tools, assistive applications, clipboard/print/download/capture policy, concurrent-session rules and exit/recovery policy.
* A rehearsal verifies that policy on the actual candidate device before the real timer starts.
* Server binds candidate, school/campus, exam, form, policy revision and one active attempt/session. Client admission is short-lived and cannot be transferred to an ordinary browser.
* Keep identity/session refresh, HTTPS, origin checks and CSRF protections working through the real managed-client and hosting/proxy path. Never fix launch or autosave by broadly disabling these protections. Use official signed client distributions, a maintained version policy and rehearsed upgrades; do not introduce a client update in the middle of an active exam.
* Use the selected client's documented configuration/version verification. A user-agent string, fullscreen event or client-side boolean is insufficient. Config Key and Browser Exam Key checks are configuration/client integration checks, not hardware attestation or proof of an uncompromised device. Modern SEB integration differs by engine/platform; follow its documented API/header capabilities rather than assuming one transport everywhere. [SEB Config Key documentation](<https://safeexambrowser.org/developer/seb-config-key.html>).
* During the active attempt, block unrelated Skoolee pages, reads, mutations, chat, notifications, account menus and other exams on the server and UI. Allow narrowly scoped exam assets, identity refresh, answer saving, approved tools/accommodations, help, submit and receipt endpoints.
* Managed-client policy must restrict opening arbitrary URLs/tabs and switching to prohibited apps for the published supported configuration. Clipboard, printing, downloads, screenshots, external displays and remote access are individually tested capabilities; unsupported restrictions cannot be advertised as enforced.
* Begin the pilot with a declared managed school-device configuration and a supported desktop OS/client build. Add macOS, tablets or BYOD only after a separate complete device test matrix. Never claim all computers are supported.

### Exit, expiry and recovery

* **Normal exit:** candidate confirms Submit and the server commits a final receipt, or the authoritative deadline expires. Only then does the normal release flow restore device access.
* Premature release links, spoofed client flags, repeat submissions, clock changes and refresh must not unlock the session or restart time. Server state and the selected client's tested release mechanism control normal release.
* A crash/restart/reconnect resumes the same frozen paper, accepted responses and deadline. An authorized session takeover immediately revokes the old writer; delayed saves from it cannot overwrite the recovered session. Concurrent writable attempts and silent answer overwrites are forbidden. Paper, delivery policy and accommodations stay pinned; any emergency policy change needs authorization, a recorded impact and renewed validation.
* The first release is online-authoritative with bounded retry/recovery. Pending local answers are shown as pending, protected according to the recovery design, and never falsely described as server-saved. Client timestamps are not sufficient to accept post-deadline work.
* Design and test automatic bounded local release at offline expiry plus an independently available emergency exit; an invigilator release is an additional path, not the only escape. Proposed UX target: release within 120 seconds of a confirmed emergency request or locally estimated expiry when the server is unreachable; validate this on the selected client before committing the supported profile. Local time may trigger safety release, but never prove that late answers are eligible. If the client cannot meet the bounded-release requirement, that managed profile must not ship. **Device released** and **submission confirmed** are separate states. Preserve pending work for audited recovery; interruption is not an automatic zero, misconduct verdict or confirmed submission.
* The recovery policy must explain before Start whether a supervised break counts against time; resume and extra time require authorized audited decisions.
* Closing/reopening or emergency exit does not erase the attempt, reset the timer or automatically regain a writable session. Restore device settings and stop optional capture when the managed session ends.

A dedicated client can enforce documented restrictions on supported configurations; it cannot guarantee control over a second device, physical camera, privileged OS compromise, power loss or every future vulnerability. No “cheat-proof” or universal lockdown claim. Camera recording is a separate, visible and permission-gated supervision policy. A school may explicitly require camera readiness before Start, with a clear preflight notice and an approved, reasoned accommodation alternative. Screen recording remains optional. This is not device lockdown; capture interruption never changes marks or blocks answer saving/submission, and browser readiness is not hardware attestation.

Official SEB documentation describes client/configuration validation and controlled post-submission exit, with a supervisor exit for technical problems. The integration must implement and test both application and client controls. [SEB integration](<https://safeexambrowser.org/developer/seb-integration.html>). Windows kiosk restrictions and permitted processes are platform features to evaluate against the exact pilot configuration. [SEB Windows manual](<https://safeexambrowser.org/windows/win_usermanual_en.html>).

## 5. Premium shared UX: seven connected spaces

Use the existing shared design system and `src/components/ui` primitives (buttons, forms, dialogs/modal, skeletons, tables, badges, confirmations, draft recovery, offline banner and app toaster). Compose exam-specific views under the established School AI components. Do not create an unrelated theme or a parallel control library.

| Space | What the person sees | Important interaction |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Welcome / Brief | A full-screen question with text/voice/upload/scan, progress and a compact editable brief | One missing decision at a time; resume and switch to structured input |
| Blueprint | Source pages, taught-objective map and a live marks/duration summary | Resolve coverage gaps and pin decisions before generation |
| Review desk | Paper preview, focused item editor and evidence/rubric drawer | Click a question to inspect evidence; targeted regenerate; explicit stale review |
| Rehearsal | Student/print preview and a short blocker list | Jump from issue to exact question/asset; repair; rerun relevant checks |
| Exam Room | Calm typography, question map, timer, save indicator, allowed tools and submit | Exam-only surface; keyboard and assistive paths preserved |
| Control room | Candidate readiness/save/incident/submission status | Human operational decisions with scope, reason and audit |
| Results / Improve | Moderation queue, learner explanation and teacher-approved practice | Inspect why a score changed; publish exact result; later formative follow-up |

Visual direction: generous reading space, strong typography hierarchy, restrained school branding and a consistent status vocabulary. Use a brief transition that explains moving from brief to paper or readiness to exam. Respect reduced motion; animations must never delay Start, obscure save status or consume exam time. No pulsing timer, promotional overlays, confetti or AI assistant answering questions during a secured exam.

Every space needs empty, loading, partial-success, error, permission, reconnect and recovery states. Use inline field/item errors and persistent action blockers; never make a transient toast the only record of an unsaved answer or failed submission. Keep student controls stable on narrow screens, zoom and long RTL content. All ordinary browsers, physical managed clients, keyboard paths and approved assistive applications need separate evidence.

## 6. Role and authority contract

| Role | May do | Cannot gain implicitly |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Teacher / author | Select authorized sources, draft brief/items/rubrics, review assigned material | Publish without assigned approval authority; read another school's content |
| Academic approver / coordinator | Approve paper/policy, assign delivery/accommodations, authorize release | Silently alter an active frozen paper or already released marks |
| Invigilator | Monitor assigned candidates, manage approved operational recovery | Read keys, mark answers or browse unrelated student records without a separate role |
| Moderator | Review evidence, approve/override scores, propose regrades | Publish outside scope or overwrite historical result versions |
| Student | Own assigned paper, permitted tools, answers, help and submit | Staff keys, hidden notes, other candidates or general Skoolee actions during an active attempt |
| Guardian | Released child feedback/actions when permitted | Live exam access, other children, unpublished keys or internal moderation notes |
| Platform/support staff | Operational diagnostics within approved support grants | Unrestricted school documents or exam answers from platform status alone |

AI may propose and explain; it may not independently publish, conclude misconduct, change authoritative marks, extend time, release a managed device or send messages to families.

## 7. Architecture and version rules

Use one school-scoped assessment record chain. Conceptual records below are contracts to map onto existing models, not claims that new tables already exist:

1. Approved source version and teacher-confirmed taught coverage.
2. Reviewed brief/blueprint revision with policy and unresolved decisions.
3. Item/evidence/key/rubric revision and reviewer decisions.
4. Immutable released paper/form/language version plus separately protected key.
5. Candidate assignment, approved accommodations and delivery-policy revision.
6. Client admission, authoritative attempt deadline and sequenced answer receipts.
7. Script matching/extraction or browser response evidence.
8. Moderation decision, immutable result version, release and appeal.
9. Reviewed intervention and later formative check.

All transitions recheck current scope/role. Treat source documents and model outputs as untrusted data, never executable instructions. Use explicit typed operations, idempotency, version checks and audited human approvals. Keep provider credentials, keys and private sources off candidate payloads and public URLs. Imported school content must not become cross-school training or a shared bank without explicit rights and authorization.

## 10. Pilot scorecard and commercial proof

Proposed recruitment target: **3–5 design-partner schools**, including Arabic-medium use and a school that needs paper/digital coexistence. This is a recruitment target, not an existing customer claim. Define participating grades, subjects, devices, accessibility needs, review ownership and data permission before each pilot.

Measure against each school's own previous workflow:

| Measure | Definition / collection |
| -- | -- |
| Preparation effort | Teacher + reviewer minutes from brief to approved paper; include corrections, not just generation time |
| Quality | Unsupported/ambiguous items identified, escaped defects after approval, correction reasons; denominators per subject/language |
| Marking effort | Minutes per reviewed script and full turnaround, with override and second-review rates |
| Reliability | Acknowledged-answer loss, duplicate finalization, pending-save incidents, recovery and device-release success |
| School operation | Invigilator/support minutes per session, readiness failure reasons, appeal resolution time |
| Adoption | Teachers returning for another assessment; students completing; buyer conversion and renewal evidence |
| Economics | Actual model/OCR/media/storage/worker/support cost per completed assessment; gross contribution under proposed package |
| Learning follow-up | Approved practice assigned/completed and later observed outcomes, without claiming causation |

**Engineering targets to validate, not reported achievements:** zero lost server-acknowledged answers and zero duplicate final receipts in the fault suite; zero successful cross-tenant/key access in authorization tests; zero silent downgrade of a managed exam. For an initial reference load, propose 500 concurrent candidates, 15-second staggered autosaves and 500 starts over one minute, with p95 save acknowledgement at or below 2 seconds and p99 at or below 5 seconds in a declared staging environment. Record payload size, device/network, worker/database/provider configuration and test duration; revise the capacity offer from evidence rather than selling these numbers as achieved.

Before public time-saving or accuracy claims, publish the sample size, collection method, relevant school context and limitations. Do not fabricate a survey, testimonial, paid contract, accuracy percentage, learning gain or market-size figure.

## 11. Packaging and investor/acquirer readiness

Test three offers with actual decision makers:

1. **Exam Studio:** approved-source authoring, rehearsal, paper/browser outputs and review workflow.

- [ ] A seeded defect is caught in rehearsal and repaired; dependent reviews/approval become stale on relevant changes.
- [ ] Approved print, browser, language and form variants are versioned, traceable and free of candidate-visible keys or reviewer notes.

- [ ] Every advertised managed-device restriction passes on each supported OS/client configuration; unsupported controls are explicitly excluded from that profile.
- [ ] Deep links, unrelated APIs, other Skoolee tabs, replayed tokens and concurrent writable sessions cannot bypass the active candidate policy.
- [ ] Clock changes, reload, crash, restart and reconnect preserve the same attempt and server deadline; out-of-order/duplicate saves preserve the newest acknowledged revision.
- [ ] Submission/expiry races return one final receipt; a lost response can be recovered without duplicate finalization. An authorized session takeover revokes the previous writer immediately, including delayed/offline save requests.

## 13. Retained detailed generation requirements

The following generation scope remains part of this feature; the flagship product adds the integrated experience and managed-delivery requirement above.

## Owned scope

* Connect an approved syllabus/source set and reviewed assessment blueprint to one generation session. Accept supported text, audio transcript, existing paper, image/PDF scan and teacher chat inputs through the app's existing intake workflows; retain provenance and source version.
* Ask only for missing decisions in a calm, resumable, conversational setup: grade/class, subject, syllabus unit/objectives, paper type, duration, total marks, question mix, language/script, accommodations and desired challenge. Show confirmed facts separately from assumptions and unknowns.
* Generate structured question items with objective tags, mark allocation, answer key, rubric/marking steps, worked explanation where requested, language, source citations and review status.
* Provide question-level edit, remove, reorder, pin, regenerate and compare actions. Regenerating one item must preserve pinned and teacher-approved items.
* Check the assembled assessment against the selected blueprint and create one immutable, versioned, reviewable paper snapshot for the downstream print and standard-browser exam features.
* Preserve model/provider, prompt/policy, source, blueprint, validator, semantic-review and teacher-approval provenance needed to reproduce and audit the exact draft.

## Required user journey

1. **Start anywhere:** teacher types, speaks, uploads/scans a syllabus or past paper, or starts from a reviewed blueprint.
2. **Understand together:** show extracted topics/objectives and source-page evidence; ask a full-screen, one-question-at-a-time follow-up only for material gaps. Teacher can correct every extracted fact.
3. **Shape the paper:** preview mark total, duration, objective coverage, item types, language, accommodations and source coverage before spending generation credits.
4. **Generate and review:** stream progress into a saved draft; show each question with its source evidence, objective, marks, answer/rubric, validator results and uncertainty. Keep teacher edits intact.
5. **Resolve blockers:** fix unsupported claims, ambiguous answers, incorrect totals, inaccessible figures, duplicate items or missing coverage; allow manual authoring when AI is unavailable.
6. **Approve exact version:** authorized staff explicitly approve the exact paper snapshot. Any subsequent content/source/key/rubric edit invalidates approval.
7. **Hand off:** send the approved immutable snapshot to paper/PDF printing or the standard-browser attempt workflow. Candidate output never contains keys, private source annotations, reviewer notes or hidden metadata.

## Quality and safety requirements

* Every factual or curriculum-specific item must cite an exact approved source page/region and objective, or be labeled unsupported and blocked from approval.
* Run deterministic validation for count, marks, duration, objective coverage, question-type constraints, required fields, duplicate/near-duplicate items, answer-key/rubric consistency, supported math, unsafe content and figure references.
* Run a separate answerability/semantic reviewer that does not see the answer key while judging whether the question is clear and answerable; compare its proposed answer with the key only after its independent assessment. A semantic pass is a review signal, never a certification.
* Editing question content invalidates dependent answer, rubric and review results. Show what became stale and require revalidation.
* If the source scan is unclear or a figure is absent/ambiguous, ask the teacher to inspect or recapture the exact page/region. Never invent diagram labels, values or curriculum facts.
* Treat difficulty as a teacher-selected target or uncalibrated estimate until sufficient moderated response data exists. Do not promise equal difficulty for parallel variants without calibration evidence.
* Keep student/candidate output and teacher-only keys/rubrics in separate authorization paths. Enforce release gates in the server/API as well as the UI.
* School content is not used for model training by default. Show provider/data-egress policy before a source is sent to an external model, and retain a manual workflow on outage, budget exhaustion or consent refusal.
* Support English, Urdu and Arabic, mixed-script math, RTL editing/preview and readable print/browser output. Never reverse math notation or answer-option order when changing direction.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Chat, voice transcript, document upload and scan entry points can begin the same resumable workflow; extracted facts retain page/region provenance and are teacher-correctable.
- [ ] The setup asks only for missing information, distinguishes confirmed facts, suggestions and unknowns, and previews the complete blueprint before generation.
- [ ] Every generated item is source/objective-linked or explicitly blocked as unsupported; the reviewer can open the cited page/region.
- [ ] Deterministic checks report actionable item-level and paper-level failures for marks, duration, objectives, formats, duplicates, key/rubric completeness, math and figures; no failed blocker can be bypassed by the UI or direct API.
- [ ] Key-blind answerability review is separate from key comparison; results and uncertainty are visible to the teacher and do not imply guaranteed correctness.
- [ ] Teacher can edit, remove, reorder, pin and regenerate a single item without losing accepted work; dependent keys/rubrics/reviews become stale when their question changes.
- [ ] A draft is saved across reload, network interruption and provider retry. Provider failure preserves teacher inputs and permits manual completion.
- [ ] No draft is distributed or released before an authorized human approves its exact version. Any later content change invalidates approval; direct API attempts to bypass this gate fail.
- [ ] Print/PDF and browser exam receive the same approved immutable paper version; verify matching version/hash and prove candidate payloads exclude answer keys, rubrics, reviewer notes and private annotations.
- [ ] Arabic/Urdu/English, RTL, mixed-script mathematics, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, narrow/mobile review and print layout are covered with representative fixtures.
- [ ] Figure-based questions are backed by a teacher-reviewed crop from the source; missing, low-quality or altered diagrams block approval until corrected.
- [ ] Parallel forms expose blueprint parity and item overlap/differences; the UI makes no equal-difficulty claim without an approved calibration method and adequate moderated data.
- [ ] Success measures are collected without inventing a baseline: teacher time from setup to approved paper, edit/override rate, unsupported-item blocker rate, invalid-item escape rate, source traceability, provider cost and manual fallback use. Numerical targets are set only after a representative baseline and school pilot.
- [ ] Release evidence includes representative subject/grade/language educator review, adversarial and failure cases, a reproducible local/browser test report, and explicitly listed external gates. Synthetic passing tests alone do not establish educational quality or school readiness.

## Experience and interface direction

Make the workflow feel like a capable assessment co-author, not a technical prompt form. Use a full-screen, warm, stepwise conversation when gathering context, with visible progress and a persistent “what we know / what needs your answer” summary. Let the teacher switch to a structured editor at any time. Use a desktop source-and-question split view and a clear sequential mobile review.

Per item, show source excerpt/page, objective, question, marks, answer, rubric, difficulty status, validation and review provenance. Use precise states: Draft, Needs source, Needs correction, Ready for review, Approved, Changed after approval. Warn before leaving only when unsaved work exists. Keep generated text visually distinct from teacher edits, but let the teacher accept the final result as their own controlled paper.

## UX states and accessibility

* **No source/blueprint:** explain the missing prerequisite and link directly to its owning feature; preserve entered setup.
* **Extraction:** show page-level progress and allow correction; never present uncertain OCR as confirmed.
* **Generating:** show item progress, save status and cancel/resume behavior; generated does not mean approved.
* **Blocker:** identify the exact item and evidence needed, with keyboard-accessible focus and a repair action.
* **Provider unavailable:** preserve the draft and offer retry/manual creation without losing state.
* **Approved:** show approver, version/hash, source set and downstream print/browser destinations.
* All core actions work by keyboard; screen readers announce progress, validation changes and save state. Follow EN/UR/AR localization and shared app design system.

## Verification examples

* Generate from a syllabus scan with a missing/blurred page and verify unsupported questions are blocked and the precise recapture/correction is offered.
* Edit a numeric value, language, question or rubric after semantic review and confirm all dependent checks become stale.
* Retry after partial provider failure and confirm pinned/approved items remain unchanged and no duplicate items are created.
* Attempt publication through the UI and direct API with a stale or unapproved version and verify rejection.
* Compare print and browser outputs and inspect student payloads for answer-key leakage.
* Review Urdu/Arabic and math in desktop, mobile, keyboard-only, screen reader and print/PDF paths.
