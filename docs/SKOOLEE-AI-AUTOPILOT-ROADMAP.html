01 The North Star — a goal-driven school OS

Today Skoolee is a set of manual modules. The leap is to make the goal the interface. The user states an outcome; AI prepares a connected package; the user reviews, edits, pins decisions and approves.

Instead of 40 screens the staff must learn, there is one line they can type or say:

  • “Set up our new academic year.”
  • “Prepare and manage the Grade 8 term exams.”
  • “Handle this month's fees and tell me the exceptions.”
  • “Create a quiz from what Class 7-B studied this week.”
  • “Arrange cover for absent teachers tomorrow.”
  • “Show where students need support and prepare follow-up.”

AI checks existing records first, asks only about missing info, conflicts, or changed policy, prepares a linked plan, validates rules/permissions, and either executes (if covered by an approved policy) or routes to a Decision Inbox for approval.

Level 1 · Assistant

Answers, drafts, suggests. Staff still perform most actions.

Level 2 · Workflow Manager — start here

Gathers info, prepares linked records, validates, executes approved changes.

Level 3 · School Autopilot

Runs recurring workflows under saved policies + limits. Decision inbox + full history.

Different features sit at different levels at the same time. Reminders can be fully automatic; final exam results always require human approval. Automation expands per feature as measured accuracy and correction-rate justify it.

Non-negotiable guardrails

  • Never invent marks without student work.
  • Never silently overwrite a newer human edit — show impact, prepare a new version.
  • Published results / sent announcements / issued invoices get versioned corrections, never deletes.
  • Every AI action is tenant-scoped (fail-closed schoolId guard) and permission-checked.
  • Findings stay distinguishable from confirmed problems.
This is the mainstream “predictable workflows + flexible agent planning” pattern (Anthropic's building-effective-agents guidance; constraint-solver for scheduling à la Google OR-Tools). Start with one coordinator and well-defined tools; add parallel agents only where they genuinely help.

04 First-time School Onboarding — AI interviews the school

A brand-new school has no records, so AI runs a guided interview. It never dumps a 60-field form — it asks in plain, batched steps, accepts chat/voice/scan/upload at every step, and can bulk-import from a scanned register, a spreadsheet, or a previous system export.

Onboarding principles

  • Progressive: only ask what's needed for the next useful outcome; defer the rest.
  • Every answer is reusable: confirmed answers become School Policies, so AI never re-asks.
  • Confidence-visible imports: OCR'd rows show a confidence badge; low-confidence rows queue for review, never silently committed.
  • Resume anywhere: setup is a draft package; closing the tab never loses entered data.

Three ways to populate a new school

  • Scan paper registers, fee sheets, timetables → OCR → reviewable drafts.
  • Upload CSV/Excel/PDF exports from a previous system.
  • Converse for small schools: AI builds records from a 10-minute chat/voice interview.

Scan a past paper

OCR → detects questions, marks, sections → produces an editable question bank entry and a paper pattern the generator can reuse.

Scan a syllabus

OCR → topic tree per subject/grade → becomes the curriculum coverage map that question generation must satisfy.

Scan marksheets / registers

OCR rows → candidate records with confidence → review queue → commit. Handwriting goes to moderation, never auto-committed.

Reality check: OCR of handwriting and messy scans is never 100%. The pipeline's job is not to be perfect — it's to get to “review & fix” fast with confidence surfaced, so staff correct a few rows instead of typing hundreds.

06 Paper / Quiz / Marking Engine — how AI “knows” the syllabus

AI doesn't guess the syllabus — it generates only from an approved curriculum source. You feed it the syllabus three possible ways; it builds a topic tree; generation and coverage checks run against that tree.

Generation rules

  • Questions trace to a topic + source — teacher sees provenance.
  • Variants (A/B/C) for anti-cheating, same difficulty profile.
  • Every paper ships with answer key + rubric; nothing publishes unreviewed.
  • Difficulty mix and topic coverage are checked by rule, not vibes.

Marking (the careful part)

  • Objective (MCQ/short) auto-scored vs. approved key.
  • Written → evidence-linked suggestions for teacher moderation.
  • Never a mark without the student's actual work attached.
  • Results are versioned; corrections leave an audit trail.

07 Secure Exam Browser (lockdown + proctoring)

For online papers you need a true lockdown environment, not just a webpage. Your repo already has a Tauri desktop shell — that is the strongest, most honest path: a desktop app can enforce OS-level restrictions a browser tab physically cannot.

Option A — Tauri lockdown app recommended

Kiosk/fullscreen, blocks app & tab switching, disables copy/paste & screenshots, single-process, camera + screen capture for proctoring. Real enforcement.

Option B — Browser extension

Lighter to ship, but bypassable; weak enforcement. Fine for low-stakes quizzes only.

Option C — Web kiosk + proctoring

Pure web; captures camera/screen and AI-flags anomalies, but cannot truly lock the OS. Good fallback where installing an app isn't possible.

sequenceDiagram autonumber participant St as Student participant SB as Secure Exam App (Tauri) participant OS as OS Lockdown layer participant Pr as Proctoring (camera+screen) participant Srv as Exam server St->>SB: Launch exam · enter code SB->>OS: Enter kiosk · block switching · disable copy/screenshot SB->>Pr: Start camera + screen capture · identity check Pr-->>Srv: Baseline face · environment scan SB->>Srv: Fetch paper (variant assigned) loop During exam St->>SB: Answer questions (autosave) Pr->>Srv: Stream events · flag anomalies (second face, tab-attempt, gaze-away) OS-->>SB: Block & log any switch attempt end St->>SB: Submit SB->>OS: Release lockdown Srv-->>St: Confirmation Srv->>Srv: Attach proctoring evidence to result (for moderation)

Enforcement (what the Tauri app can really do)

  • Fullscreen kiosk; intercept/kill Alt-Tab, Cmd-Tab, Win key, mission control.
  • Disable clipboard, right-click, print-screen; block new windows & external links.
  • Detect virtual machines / second displays / screen-recorders and refuse to start.
  • Camera + screen stream to proctor; local buffer if network drops.

Honesty + ethics guardrails

  • AI flags are evidence for a human, never auto-fail.
  • Consent + privacy notice before camera starts; data retention policy.
  • Accessibility & accommodations path (extra time, screen reader exemptions).
  • Graceful fallback to web-kiosk when a student can't install the app.
No lockdown is unbreakable (a second phone/camera always exists). Position it as strong deterrence + evidence capture + human review, and lean on question variants + time limits + randomized order to blunt collusion. That framing sells better than claiming “uncheatable.”

09 UX Principles (so no one is afraid of it)

  • One decision at a time. Never a wall of fields; AI asks the single next thing it needs.
  • Draft, not do. Default is “prepared for your approval,” so staff feel in control from day one.
  • Every artifact has View · Edit · Explain · Approve · History. No black boxes.
  • “Keep this fixed.” Human edits become constraints, never discarded on regenerate.
  • Confidence is visible. OCR/AI outputs show how sure they are; low confidence routes to review.
  • Bilingual by default (Arabic + English, RTL-aware) — essential for Riyadh.
  • Works on a phone. Scan, approve, resolve a conflict from the hallway.
  • Reversible. Versioned corrections, never silent deletes; staff trust what they can undo.
The emotional design goal: a principal who has never used “AI” should feel like they hired a competent assistant — one that prepares the work, explains itself, and waits for the nod.

11 Will this attract people? — LEAP 2027 Riyadh read

Short answer: yes, the concept is well-aimed for this market and this moment — if you lead with one undeniable workflow and the Saudi-specific fit, not a feature list.

Vision 2030Education + AI are national priorities; buyers expect AI, don't fear it.
Arabic-firstRTL + Arabic AI is a real moat vs. generic Western SIS tools.
Time-savedAdmin overload is universal; “prepare the work” is a visceral pitch.
LEAP stageOne of the largest tech events globally — huge top-of-funnel if the demo lands.

Why it can attract people

  • The pitch is a feeling, not features: “Describe the outcome, we prepare it, you approve.” Every overworked administrator gets it in one sentence.
  • Saudi fit: Arabic/RTL, local curriculum boards, Vision 2030 AI narrative, Hijri calendar, SAR fees — these are table stakes competitors often miss.
  • Secure exam browser is a concrete, boardroom-friendly differentiator (exam integrity is a hot topic).
  • Trust-first UX (approve, explain, undo) answers the #1 objection to “AI in schools.”

What would make it NOT attract people risks

  • Demo breadth over depth. Showing 20 half-working AI buttons reads as a toy. Show one flawless flow.
  • “Uncheatable” claims. Over-promising on the secure browser invites easy rebuttals. Sell deterrence + evidence.
  • No Arabic / no local curriculum. A generic English demo in Riyadh signals “not built for us.”
  • No proof it's safe. Without visible approval/version/audit, decision-makers won't trust AI near grades & money.
  • Pricing & data residency unanswered. KSA buyers will ask where data lives — have a PDPL/residency answer.
LEAP demo recipe: (1) Principal says one sentence → (2) AI asks ONE smart question → (3) a full exam package appears, editable → (4) resolve one conflict → (5) approve → (6) flash the secure exam browser locking down. 90 seconds. In Arabic. That is a booth-stopping demo.

Honest verdict

The idea is strong and timely — goal-driven, trust-first, Arabic-native school automation is genuinely differentiated and lands squarely in Saudi Arabia's 2030 education-tech push. Attraction at LEAP will depend far more on demo craft and local fit than on how many modules are automated. Build narrow and deep: one flawless, Arabic, end-to-end flow with the secure-browser flourish will out-pull a broad-but-shallow product every time. Pair it with a credible trust/safety/data-residency story and you have something a Riyadh school leader will remember.
